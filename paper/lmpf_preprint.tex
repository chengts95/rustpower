\begin{abstract}
We present a Levenberg--Marquardt power flow (LMPF) engine in which the
Jacobian assembly cost is eliminated by reusing the symbolic operator of a
production Newton--Raphson solver, and the normal-equation system is
factorized as a quasi-definite symmetric system by QDLDL. Assembly is
12--42$\times$ faster than COO/spgemm-based paths and is no longer a
measurable share of runtime: a full LMPF solve costs 174\,\textmu s on the
39-bus system and about 200\,ms on the 9241-bus system, with the sparse
factorization itself the only remaining cost.
Equipped with factorization-accurate accounting, we re-examine the
``ill-conditioned power flow'' literature under a controlled dual-start
protocol. The three RTE snapshots widely treated as hard are solved by a
dc-started Newton--Raphson in 6--8 iterations; the flagship case2383wp,
reported to diverge with $\kappa = 2.3\times10^{12}$, converges in 4
iterations from both starts with a measured $\kappa(J^*) = 6.0\times10^{5}$.
A parameter-free realization of the trust-region LM --- one probe
factorization at the first iteration measures the merit retention of the
undamped Gauss--Newton step and sets both the initial radius and the damping
level --- converges from flat starts where every parameter-free competitor
fails, in 19--25 total factorizations on the RTE cases, fewer than the best
hand-tuned fixed radius. On the Tripathy 11-bus system we certify that no
solution exists at the reported operating point (the fold lies at
$\alpha \approx 0.643$) and compute its least-squares point, settling a
forty-year conflict in favor of Iwamoto's judgment. We conclude that LMPF's
productive role is not rescuing Newton's method but delivering least-squares
diagnostics of the power flow equations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Power flow is folklore-solved. Yet since Iwamoto and Tamura's optimal
multiplier method~\cite{iwamoto1981} and the Newton-like method of Tripathy
et al.~\cite{tripathy1982}, the literature has kept reporting Newton
failures on ``ill-conditioned'' systems and remedies built on the
Levenberg--Marquardt (LM) idea~\cite{levenberg1944,marquardt1963}, most
recently a fuzzy-logic three-step LM~\cite{derakhshandeh2018}.

Three confounds recur in this literature, and to our knowledge have never
been controlled simultaneously. First, costs are reported in outer
iterations, although one outer iteration of an LM variant may hide anywhere
from one to hundreds of sparse factorizations. Second, starts are not
controlled: flat starts and dc starts are mixed across tables, although the
initial point, not the solver, decides most reported failures. Third,
implementations are slow enough that measured runtimes reflect COO
reassembly and general-purpose LU rather than the mathematics.

This paper removes the third confound with an engine, and the first two
with a protocol; what remains of ``ill-conditioned power flow'' is then
small, measurable, and --- we argue --- more interesting than the folklore.
Our contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An LMPF engine with eliminated assembly cost}
  (Section~3). The symbolic Jacobian operator of a production
  Newton--Raphson solver --- Ybus-driven, pre-sorted CSC --- transfers
  verbatim to LMPF; the normal-equation pattern is analyzed once per
  topology; numerical assembly is a straight-line pass with direct offset
  writes. Assembly is 12--42$\times$ faster than COO/spgemm paths and the
  symmetric QDLDL factorization of the quasi-definite system beats
  general-purpose LU on identical iterates.
\item \textbf{A parameter-free trust-region realization that wastes no
  factorizations} (Section~5.5). Adaptive trust-region strategies for
  power-flow LM have been studied before~\cite{nocedal2006,csee_adaptive_lm};
  we do not claim the concept. Our contribution is a realization whose
  entire start-up cost is \emph{one} probe factorization: the merit
  retention of the undamped Gauss--Newton step classifies the start across
  a five-order-of-magnitude margin, and doubles as the damping scale,
  so no factorizations are burned on damping search. The result beats the
  best hand-tuned fixed radius we could find (19--25 vs.\ 44--48
  factorizations on the RTE cases).
\item \textbf{A controlled numerical re-examination} (Sections~4, 5.2--5.4).
  Under a dual-start protocol with factorization accounting and bit-level
  cross-validation against pandapower, today's so-called ill-conditioned
  scenarios yield directly to a dc-started Newton--Raphson, and prior
  performance claims do not reproduce.
\item \textbf{A repositioning of LMPF} (Section~6). LMPF's unique
  deliverable is the stationary point of the merit $\tfrac12\|F\|^2$ where
  Newton has nothing to say: a numerical certificate of infeasibility and a
  localization of conflicting specifications.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Problem Setup}

\subsection{Power Flow Equations and Newton--Raphson}

We use the standard mismatch formulation $F(x) = 0$ in polar coordinates:
active and reactive power mismatches at PQ buses, active mismatches at PV
buses, with state $x = (\theta, |V|)$. Buses are ordered
$[\mathrm{PQ} \,|\, \mathrm{PV} \,|\, \mathrm{slack}]$ throughout, and the
reduced Jacobian $J = \partial F / \partial x$ has $2n_{\mathrm{PQ}} +
n_{\mathrm{PV}}$ rows and columns. Newton--Raphson solves
$J\,\delta = -F$ and updates $x \leftarrow x + \delta$; it converges
quadratically inside its basin and says nothing outside it.

\subsection{The Levenberg--Marquardt Family}

All LM variants we consider share one umbrella: they iterate on the merit
\begin{equation}
  f(x) \;=\; \tfrac12 \|F(x)\|^2
\end{equation}
by solving the damped normal equations
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(J^{\top} J + \mu D\bigr)\,\delta \;=\; -J^{\top} F,
  \label{eq:lm}
\end{equation}
where $D$ is a diagonal metric (we use the polar metric
$D = \mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1)$ on angles and magnitudes). Variants differ
only in two choices: how $\mu$ is adapted, and how many right-hand sides
each factorization serves.

\paragraph{Residual-scaled damping.} Setting $\mu$ proportional to the
residual level, $\gamma = \|F\|^2$, makes the method parameter-free; the
fuzzy-logic three-step scheme of~\cite{derakhshandeh2018} belongs to this
branch. We show in Section~5.5 that this branch pays linear convergence
rates in the filtered subspace.

\paragraph{Trust-region damping.} The trust-region LM constrains
$\|\delta\|_D \le \Delta$ and adapts both $\mu$ and the radius $\Delta$ from
the ratio of actual to predicted reduction~\cite{nocedal2006}. Adaptive
choices of the initial radius have appeared in the power-flow
literature~\cite{csee_adaptive_lm}; our realization is described in
Section~5.5.

\paragraph{Composite steps.} Several right-hand sides can share one
factorization (chord steps, multi-step corrections). This is where
outer-iteration accounting breaks down, and why we count factorizations.

\subsection{DC Initialization}

The dc model linearizes the angle channel: one linear solve
$B'\,\theta = P$ yields angles that are exact up to phase-shifter
injections and losses. Voltage magnitudes remain flat at $1.0$ p.u.; dc
initialization fixes the angle channel and cannot fix the voltage channel.
In our measurements the dc angle correction on the RTE cases is
$0.05$--$0.08$ radians at base loading --- small in absolute terms, decisive
in whether the first Newton step stays in the basin.

\section{The LMPF Engine}

\subsection{Symbolic Jacobian Operator Reuse}

The production Newton--Raphson solver assembles the reduced Jacobian by a
symbolic operator derived from the Ybus structure: the Ybus is pre-sorted,
and each Jacobian column's nonzero positions are computed symbolically once
per topology. This operator transfers verbatim to LMPF --- the $J$ in
\eqref{eq:lm} is the same matrix --- so the entire sparsity analysis of the
LM iteration is inherited, not rebuilt.

\subsection{Direct-Offset Fill}

Diagonal entries and per-column starting offsets are computed directly from
the Ybus base addresses; no COO triplet list, no sorting pass, no scatter.
Assembly is a straight-line pass over the precomputed pattern. A $\mu$ retry
touches only the diagonal $\mu$ slots and never triggers reassembly.

\subsection{Normal-Equation Symbolic Analysis and Symmetric Factorization}

We do not form $J^{\top}J$ numerically. The augmented system
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} \mu D & J^{\top} \\ J & -I \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \delta \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
  \;=\;
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -F \end{bmatrix}
  \label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
is quasi-definite ($\mu D \succ 0$, $-I \prec 0$), hence a no-pivot
LDL$^{\top}$ factorization is stable. Its nonzero pattern is built
symbolically from the Jacobian pattern alone, and the row-oriented fill
writes only the upper triangle: the transpose $J^{\top}$ is emitted row by
row in a single pass, with no materialized $J$ block, no transpose scatter,
and no column copies. LU backends such as KLU~\cite{klu2010} require the
full matrix and cannot exploit this layout; QDLDL~\cite{qdldl} factorizes
the upper triangle directly and beats KLU on the identical augmented
system by up to $3\times$ on 9k-bus systems
(Table~\ref{tab:backend}).

\subsection{Factorization Reuse (resolve)}

The solver interface exposes a pure triangular resolve, so composite-step
methods (one factorization, several right-hand sides) ride on the same
factorization. This is the engine-level support that makes
factorization-accurate accounting meaningful: every factorization is an
explicit, countable event.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}

Three confounds have never been controlled simultaneously in this
literature; our protocol controls all three.

\subsection{Dual-Start Protocol}

Every case is run from a flat start and from a dc start under identical
tolerances and identical inputs. Inputs and results are cross-validated
against pandapower~\cite{pandapower2018} on the same network model: the
Ybus, injections, and start points match bit-for-bit, and converged
voltages agree to $\max|\Delta V| \sim 10^{-13}$.

\subsection{Factorization Accounting}

Costs are reported in factorizations (sparse linear solves), not outer
iterations. An outer iteration is not a unit of work: we exhibit below a
run where 15 outer iterations perform 1\,214 factorizations.

\subsection{Merit Certificate (Existence)}

The merit value $f(x^*) = \tfrac12\|F(x^*)\|^2$ and its gradient at
convergence distinguish ``no solution exists'' (a stationary point with a
nonzero merit floor) from ``solution not reached''. LM converging to a
nonzero floor is a certificate, not a failure mode.

\subsection{Condition-Number Certificate (Near-Singularity)}

We measure $\kappa(J^*) = \sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ of the reduced
Jacobian at the solution by power and inverse-power iteration.
Ill-conditioning is a measured property of a point, not a label on a
network.

\subsection{Fold Location by Continuation}

A Newton corrector chained from the previous solution traces the solution
branch in the loading factor $\alpha$ and brackets the fold. This
separates ``beyond the nose (unsolvable)'' from ``outside the basin
(reachable)'' --- two situations the literature has systematically
conflated.

All tables in Section~5 are generated under this protocol, end to end, by
two commands: a pandapower export script that captures the exact solver
inputs, and the sweep binary that replays them. No case data is archived;
every number regenerates from the public case definitions.

One row deserves emphasis beyond speed. On case6515rte from a flat start,
the normal-equation path with KLU \emph{as deployed} --- refactorization
reusing the pivot order of the previous iterate --- failed to converge in
all 7 measured rounds, residual stalled at $1.5\times10^{3}$, while every
augmented-path run converged identically (24 outer iterations / 44
factorizations). The failure is silent: KLU's refactor recycles the pivot
order chosen for earlier values, on the squared-condition normal equation
the recycled pivots lose all accuracy, yet no singularity is reported and
the status-based fallback never fires (zero fallbacks in our counters).
Step control cannot rescue a poisoned direction either: every trial step
the trust region evaluates is generated by the same bad factors, and the
run stalls at a residual of $1.5\times10^{3}$ with rejections exhausted.
Forcing a fresh factorization at every iteration makes KLU converge to the
identical trajectory (voltage agreement $4.3\times10^{-12}$) --- at
2\,242\,ms of linear solve time versus QDLDL's 391\,ms, a further
$5.7\times$ penalty on top of the assembly difference. On ill-conditioned
inputs KLU therefore offers a choice between fast-and-silently-wrong
(refactor) and correct-but-slow (fresh factorization); the quasi-definite
augmented form needs no pivoting for stability, so the LDL$^{\top}$ path
is the fast and the correct choice at once.

\subsection{Autopsy: case2383wp}

Prior work~\cite{derakhshandeh2018} reported Newton divergence on this
flagship case and a condition number $\kappa = 2.3\times10^{12}$. With a
correct Newton--Raphson, case2383wp converges in 4 iterations from both
flat and dc starts --- under both phase-shifter sign conventions, which we
tested separately because the published data ambiguity flips the sign.
Measured at the solution, $\kappa(J^*) = 6.0\times10^{5}$ at base loading,
rising to $2.8\times10^{6}$ near the fold at $\alpha \approx 1.88$ located
by continuation. The system is mildly conditioned at its operating point
and genuinely near-singular only at the fold, exactly as power-flow theory
says every system is. The reported $\kappa = 2.3\times10^{12}$ does not
reproduce.

\subsection{Autopsy: the Tripathy 11-Bus System}

The canonical ill-conditioned system of~\cite{tripathy1982} is the one case
in our study where the pathology is real --- and it is not a conditioning
pathology. Continuation locates the fold at $\alpha \approx 0.643$:
\emph{below} nominal load. No power flow solution exists at the reported
operating point, and no solver can converge to one. GN-LM converges from
both starts to the same stationary point of the merit,
$\tfrac12\|F\|^2 = 8.917\times10^{-4}$. This settles a forty-year conflict
in favor of Iwamoto's judgment~\cite{iwamoto1981}: the ``solution''
tabulated in a well-known textbook is the solution of a different
(constant-impedance) problem. The least-squares point, not a converged
flag, is the correct output here --- which is precisely the role we claim
for LMPF in Section~6.

\subsection{LM Variants under Honest Accounting}

We now compare the LM family on the RTE cases under factorization
accounting: residual-scaled damping (TS, the parameter-free branch
of~\cite{derakhshandeh2018}), trust region with a hand-tuned fixed initial
radius, and our parameter-free adaptive trust region. All run the same
engine, the same merit, and the same tolerances; the only difference is
step control. Entries are outer iterations / factorizations; for TS, whose
composite fires three shots through one shared factorization, the second
entry is factorizations\,+\,triangular resolves (a resolve costs roughly a
seventh of a factorization here).

\paragraph{The probe.} One extra factorization at the first iteration takes
a nearly undamped Gauss--Newton step $d$ ($\mu = 10^{-6}$) and measures the
\emph{merit retention}
\begin{equation}
  r \;=\; f(x_0 + d) \,/\, f(x_0).
\end{equation}
Healthy starts produce $r \in [0.003,\,0.14]$; diverging flat starts on
stressed systems produce $r \in [200,\,10^4]$. The five-order-of-magnitude
margin makes the classification insensitive to the threshold $\kappa$
(default 5, interpreted as the tolerated merit amplification along the
undamped direction). If $r \le \kappa$, the radius is retired and the
iteration is plain damped Newton, at the cost of exactly one extra
factorization per solve. If $r > \kappa$, the initial radius is set to
$\|d\|\sqrt{\kappa/r}$ --- the step length at which the quadratic
amplification reaches the tolerated level --- and the damping is
warm-started at $\mu = r$: the retention ratio is itself the observable
scale of the bad curvature the damping must overcome, so the first clamped
step is reached in $O(1)$ solves rather than after $\sim$30 wasted
factorizations of doubling search. Adaptive initial radii are not new in
this literature~\cite{csee_adaptive_lm}; the one-probe realization with
exact accounting, and the use of the retention ratio as the damping scale,
are our engineering contribution.

Three observations. First, on healthy systems the adaptive variant is plain
damped Newton plus one factorization: identical outer counts to NR-class
convergence, with the probe the only tax. Second, on the RTE flat starts,
where NR, plain GN, and steepest-descent damping all fail, the adaptive
trust region converges in 19--25 total factorizations --- fewer than the
best hand-tuned radius, because the tuned radius still pays a doubling
search for $\mu$ at the first iteration and the probe does not. Third, the
residual-scaled branch converges parameter-free but pays linear rates in
the filtered subspace: 152--245 true factorizations, still 6--10$\times$
behind even with its resolves counted as free.
The composite itself is not the problem. A fresh-J variant that
re-assembles and re-factorizes at every shot --- abandoning the
amortization entirely --- lands within 2\% of the frozen-J outer count on
every system tested, healthy or stressed (Table~\ref{tab:variants}): the
frozen Jacobian is vindicated outright, and the amortization is a genuine
free lunch, three shots for one factorization along an identical
trajectory. The bottleneck is the scalar $\gamma = \|F\|^2$ itself: one
damping knob cannot fit the well-conditioned bulk and the near-singular
subspace at once, and as the merit falls the knob turns the wrong way for
exactly the directions that need damping most. Step control needs two
independent knobs --- a radius for step length and $\mu$ for direction ---
which is precisely what the trust region provides.

The accounting itself is a result. In one run on case6495rte, the
single-step residual-scaled variant reports 15 outer iterations while
performing 1\,214 true factorizations of damping retry (every $\gamma$
escalation re-factorizes). Outer-iteration reporting is not an
acceptable cost metric for this family; the literature's iteration tables
do not translate into work.

\section{The Role of LMPF}

\subsection{Least-Squares Semantics}

When a solution exists and the start is sane, dc+NR ends the discussion ---
faster, simpler, and with no damping parameters at all. LMPF's unique
deliverable is the stationary point of $\tfrac12\|F\|^2$ where NR has
nothing to say: beyond the fold, or outside every basin.

\subsection{Infeasibility as Information}

The least-squares point certifies infeasibility numerically, and its
residual pattern localizes the conflicting specifications --- which buses,
which injections, which constraints fight each other. This is actionable
information for the engineer who specified the impossible case, and it is
historically anticipated by Iwamoto's optimal-multiplier
judgment~\cite{iwamoto1981}: a non-converged run is data, not noise.

\subsection{Re-examining ``Ill-Conditioned Power Flow''}

Our autopsies suggest the term has been doing duty for at least three
distinct phenomena: nonexistence beyond a fold (Tripathy's 11-bus),
initialization outside the basin (the RTE snapshots, settled by dc start),
and genuine near-singularity at a fold (every system, including
case2383wp). Only the third is a conditioning property, and it is
universal. We call for the term to be re-founded on the measurable
certificates of Section~4 --- existence, conditioning, initialization ---
as the reporting standard for any future claim of an ill-conditioned power
flow.

\section{Conclusion}

Fast instrumentation plus honest accounting turns a folklore problem into a
settled one. With assembly eliminated and factorizations counted, the
hardest public cases yield to a dc-started Newton; the one genuinely
pathological case is pathological because it has no solution; and LMPF,
freed from the rescue narrative, becomes what it mathematically is: the
instrument that computes the least-squares point of the power flow
equations and reads out what the specifications were fighting about.
